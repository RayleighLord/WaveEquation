# AGENTS.md

## Product

This is a client-only Vite application for exploring the one-dimensional homogeneous wave equation. It must remain deployable as a static GitHub Pages site without a backend.

The product fixes wave speed to $c=1$. Use this convention everywhere in the editor, rendered mathematics, and product documentation:

$$
u_{tt}-u_{xx}=0.
$$

with initial data $u(x,0)=f(x)$ and $u_t(x,0)=g(x)$. Product-facing spatial domains are the infinite line, $[a,\infty)$, and $[a,b]$; the right-bounded half-line is not exposed. Every finite endpoint independently accepts time-dependent Dirichlet data $u=h(t)$ or Neumann data $u_x=q(t)$. PDE forcing, damping, variable wave speed, Robin data, periodic data, accounts, persistence, and server-side services are outside this product. The numerical core may retain generic-$c$ and right-bounded-half-line support as an internal capability, but no UI path or shipped preset may expose it.

The default is a Gaussian displacement with zero velocity on the infinite line, $T=8$, and a view from $x=-6$ through $x=6$. Every accepted problem change and Restart returns to a paused $t=0$ state; only an explicit Play or Replay action advances time. Once played, the Gaussian should visibly split into equal left- and right-traveling pulses. Every shipped example has complete infinite, $[a,\infty)$, and finite variants; changing the domain while a preset is active replaces the entire coherent problem instead of patching only its domain field.

## Architecture And Numerical Invariants

- Keep semantic markup and application lifecycle in `src/app.ts`, accepted draft state and scheduling in `src/ui/`, parsing, quadrature, characteristics, and solution logic in `src/math/`, revision-tagged work in `src/workers/`, and rendering in `src/plot/`. Shared problem, grid, playback, and trace contracts belong in `src/types.ts`.
- Parse expressions into a whitelisted AST. Initial expressions accept only `x`; boundary expressions accept only `t`; scalar fields $T,x_{\min},x_{\max},a,b$ and piece bounds accept constant-only expressions. Scalar expressions support the same constants and elementary functions, including `pi`, `π`, `e`, and `sin`, but reject variables and non-finite results. Never use `eval`, `new Function`, generated JavaScript, or another runtime-code-generation shortcut. Preserve ordinary arithmetic precedence and right-associative exponentiation.
- Initial displacement and velocity are each ordered lists of at most 16 pieces. Finite-domain pieces cover the whole interval without gaps or overlaps. Omitted unbounded-domain regions evaluate to zero. Reject non-finite bounds and values, non-positive widths, unknown identifiers, invalid finite intervals, and $c\le0$.
- Keep an invalid draft editable while the last accepted surface and snapshot remain visible. Worker request, result, and error messages remain revision tagged; never accept stale responses. Direct Dirichlet corner contradictions are errors. Higher-order compatibility concerns are warnings so weak/discontinuous solutions remain explorable.
- Resolution follows the accepted physical window instead of fixed counts. Preserve $h_x=12/256$ and target $h_t=0.05$, keeping a traveling feature near one retained spatial cell per row: round rendered interval counts upward in groups of eight, with a $256\times80$ screen-density floor and caps of 512 spatial and 400 temporal intervals. Accepted worker grids contain twice the spatial intervals but retain the adaptive time rows directly; renderers therefore use exact spatial indices $0,2,4,\ldots$ and every accepted time row, including both endpoints. Cut surfaces use 512 spatial intervals and additionally require $R_t\ge T R_x/(x_{\max}-x_{\min})$ up to the temporal cap. Keep the accepted surface range fixed while scrubbing. Report warnings, worker timing, and the maximum reflection count; reject paths requiring more than 64 complete reflections.
- Choose five pleasant vertical-axis ticks from four equal rounded intervals. Nonnegative solutions use an exact $u_{\min}=0$ floor and an adaptive ceiling such as $1$, $1.2$, or $1.6$; all-negative ranges end at zero, and genuinely signed waves retain outward-rounded negative and positive bounds. Ignore only scale-relative floating-point sign noise and never alter or clip the solution samples.
- Use $u(x,t)=F(x-ct)+G(x+ct)$ with
  $$
  F_0=\frac{f}{2}-\frac{1}{2c}\int g,\qquad
  G_0=\frac{f}{2}+\frac{1}{2c}\int g.
  $$
  Continue these functions at boundaries with the exact Dirichlet/Neumann reflection relations documented in the implementation plan. Antiderivatives use refinement-checked, piece-aware quadrature; do not silently replace them with frame-dependent sampling.
- Characteristics travel with slopes $dx/dt=\pm c$, reflect at every physical boundary, and terminate exactly at $t=0$. A selected trace may retain surface samples internally, but presentation draws only the two complete broken rays on the $x$-$t$ floor, plus boundary hits, initial-line footpoints, and the selected point; never draw lifted characteristic paths over the wave surface.

## Rendering, Interaction, And Accessibility

- The visualization is the protagonist: a dark full-viewport desktop stage with the 3D $u(x,t)$ surface occupying about 63% of the height and the synchronized $u$-versus-$x$ snapshot below. On desktop, center the snapshot at exactly `50%` of the available stage width (three quarters of its former two-thirds width); compact layouts use a full-width snapshot and document flow rather than overlapping panels. Keep controls compact and floating.
- Use a fixed turquoise, double-sided, diffusely lit (`MeshLambertMaterial`) surface with no value colormap. The current visual trial is completely opaque at opacity `1`, `transparent: false`, `depthWrite: true`, and `forceSinglePass: true`. Keep WebGL multisampling disabled and render its drawing buffer at `0.9` of the capped device-pixel ratio while CSS2D mathematics remains full-resolution: the dense smooth geometry already defines the shape, while full-pixel MSAA creates release-blocking software-raster Long Tasks in headless Chromium. The translucent double-sided time plane also uses one transparent pass. Reveal the graph through smooth, deliberately bright lighting rather than a surface lattice or wireframe: use a strong soft upper key, a bright hemisphere light with a nonblack ground color, and a weaker lower directional fill so the underside remains shaded but readable when the camera moves below the $x$-$t$ plane. The selected-time plane is translucent gold, directly draggable, and owns a bright surface intersection. Keep the snapshot curve turquoise. Render $\eta$ characteristics in pleasant red `#e76f67` and $\xi$ characteristics in high-contrast purple `#a970ff`, always with explicit KaTeX names so meaning never relies on color alone. Keep hit, footpoint, and selected-point spheres mathematically on the $x$-$t$ floor but render their opaque color in the final transparent overlay pass (`transparent: true`, opacity `1`, `depthTest: false`, `depthWrite: false`) so the surface cannot overpaint or half-clip them. Present characteristic annotations only as $\eta=\text{value}$ or $\xi=\text{value}$ (optionally following a boundary name); never expand either symbol as a formula in visible or accessible text.
- Preserve true spatial jump discontinuities instead of smoothing them through ramp triangles. Topology detection examines spatial neighbors only; a coarse but smooth time evolution must never be misclassified as a jump. Accepted grids whose maximum spatial neighbor jump is at least `20%` of their sampled range use the discontinuity-aware cut-cell topology: merged flat plateau strips, independent vertices/normals, and explicit slanted vertical jump walls rendered with the uniform unlit turquoise wall material. The two-pass builder preallocates exact typed buffers and merges equal-height runs so high temporal resolution does not create one quad per raw cell. Smooth solutions retain the indexed Lambert height field. The detector remains data-driven so edited/custom square waves keep discontinuity-aware treatment.
- Whenever the accepted domain has a physical finite endpoint, mark it consistently in both plots. The snapshot uses a retained white circle sampled on the current profile at $u(x_{\mathrm{boundary}},t)$; the 3D view uses a retained thick white world-width trace following the complete surface curve $u(x_{\mathrm{boundary}},t)$ through time. A half-line has one marker/trace, a finite interval has two, and the infinite line has none. Boundary presentation follows accepted state and must not change for an invalid draft.
- Use the shared modestly darker blue-turquoise `#4fcbd3` for the surface and snapshot curve. The time plane itself is the drag target; do not add a cube or other bulky handle.
- Keep the 3D coordinate convention visually aligned with the supplied low-oblique reference perspective and its camera side: time $t$ runs along world $X$, space $x$ recedes in world $Z$, and $u(x,t)$ is vertical. The default camera shows the $u$ axis vertically at the left and $x$ crossing the foreground. Place the time axis on the opposite spatial edge, physically $x=x_{\min}$, so increasing $t$ and its label project on the left. Changing the camera must not silently swap these semantic axes or mirror the default back to the former side. Empty-scene orbiting must cross freely into the lower hemisphere so the double-sided surface can be inspected from below $u=0$; constrain only small symmetric guards around the two polar singularities, and keep Reset view tied to the established default camera and target.
- Keep the 3D $u$-axis tick numbers clearly offset to the left of its axis line. Place the 3D $t$-axis tick numbers below the floor axis with the same vertical convention as the $x$ ticks, while anchoring each complete value on the spatially left side so it retains visible clearance from the line. At the shared 3D origin, retain the $t=0$ grid line and origin but elide its redundant $t$-axis label so it cannot overlap the visible $u=0$ label; in the default view the visible $t$ labels therefore begin at $0.5$. In the snapshot, follow the actual vertical $u$ axis—at the central $x=0$ position whenever zero is visible, and at the applicable boundary otherwise—with numeric $u$ ticks on its left and the $u(x,t)$ axis name on its right whenever it fits. Only a view whose axis lies at the right boundary may fall back to putting the name on the left to prevent clipping. Keep both sides adjacent to the axis and contained at compact widths rather than pinning either to the plot border.
- Draw a visible arrowhead at the positive end of every coordinate axis: all three axes in the 3D scene and both axes in the snapshot. In the snapshot, extend both the positive $x$-axis and $u$-axis tips 16 px beyond their final data/grid coordinates and place the $x$ name 3 px before its new tip; the curve, final numeric tick, grid, and hit area still end at the mathematical limits. Place $u(x,t)$ at a preferred 18 px gap to the right of a central or left-boundary $u$ axis, clamping it inside the SVG on compact widths; only a physical right-boundary axis may fall back to a matching 18 px gap on the left. Arrowheads move with their axes and must remain legible without changing the mathematical axis direction.
- Typeset every mathematical display label and number with KaTeX: 3D/SVG axis names and ticks, time ticks and outputs, characteristic values, formula hints, and variable annotations. Ordinary prose and editable native input/select values remain native for usability and accessibility.
- Plot tick numbers, axis names, and 3D annotations use the established $1.5\times$ presentation scale. The time-controller tick numerals and current-$t$ output are also enlarged to exactly $1.5\times$ their former controller sizes; do not collapse them back to generic chrome typography. Keep foreign-object/CSS2D bounds large enough that no KaTeX label is clipped at desktop or compact widths.
- The page, app shell, WebGL clear, plot stage, and plot surfaces are pure black. Plot containers have no borders, radii, shadows, colored gradients, or vignette overlays; only floating controls retain glass panels. Desktop leaves a modest left gutter for the editor and compact layouts reset to full-width flow.
- Floating chrome follows the HeatEquation palette rather than the plot color: neutral near-black navy panels, lavender-blue borders, light neutral text, and restrained `#00d3ff` only for active accents. Keep the editor approximately `17.5–19.5rem` wide with dense 36-pixel fields while retaining 44-pixel primary touch targets.
- The visible time controller is one compact row—playback, slider/ticks, and the current $t$ output. Always render the visible current time, its output label, and the slider value text with exactly three decimal places. Keep the KaTeX time output in a fixed `6.25rem` column so its content cannot resize the output or shift the slider. The snapshot plot has no redundant visible “Selected time”, “Snapshot”, or duplicate time heading; its hidden semantic title and live shared time control remain accessible.
- Both plots use aligned pleasant grids. Decimal axes select steps from $1,2,2.5,5\times10^n$; whenever the accepted spatial domain/view sources use `pi` or $\pi$, both spatial axes switch to familiar rational-$\pi$ ticks, and the 3D time axis switches independently when $T$ uses `pi`. The same spatial notation formats visible $\eta$/$\xi$ constants and initial-line footpoint descriptions, without expanding either characteristic symbol. The 3D floor retains its modest density while the snapshot targets roughly twice as many $x$ intervals. Only the duplicate visible $t=0$ label is elided at the shared 3D origin.
- Keep the compact problem card purely symbolic: $u_{tt}-u_{xx}=0$, $T$, $a,b$, $f,g$, and the selected $h_L,q_L,h_R,q_R$ boundary symbols. Put both finite-interval boundary conditions on one row. Place the card in the left problem-control stack directly between the “Wave Equation” title row and the editor panel; it remains visible when the editor panel is collapsed. Its mathematics uses the established enlarged scale—exactly `1.2` times the original left-stack `clamp()` values on both desktop and compact layouts—and must still fit without clipping. Compact layouts keep the same title-formula-editor order in full-width document flow before the plots. The same symbols must appear beside their corresponding native editor fields; never substitute current numeric values or expressions into this summary card.
- One selected-time state drives the plane, intersection, snapshot, slider, output, and playback. Plane drag and slider input pause playback. Empty-scene drag orbits the camera. Playback traverses $0\le t\le T$ in about 12 seconds, ends in Replay state, and does not consume hidden-page time.
- Opening characteristics pauses playback and reveals only a compact point-entry form: safe constant-expression fields for $x_*$ and $t_*$, a Trace action, live range validation, and a Choose-on-curve action. Keep text-capable mobile keyboards and accept sources such as `pi / 2`, `log(2)`, `sqrt(2)`, powers, and literal `π`, while rejecting variables and non-finite values. Values synchronized from a pi-aware plot must remain parser-roundtrippable ASCII sources such as `-3 * pi / 2`, never presentation-only text such as `−3π/2`. Pointer, touch, and keyboard curve selection remain available and synchronize the entry values. Preserve the selected $x_*$ when playback resumes and recompute the trace at the new time. The backward-left stored branch is red $\eta$ and the backward-right stored branch is purple $\xi$; keep these selected-point family names across reflected broken rays. Label their constants at floor events and initial footpoints instead of using a generic $x_0$, without displaying symbolic expansions of $\eta$ or $\xi$. Keep trace presentation inside the plots and their accessible descriptions; do not restore a separate “Domain of dependence” summary box.
- The problem editor is a compact accordion with no wave-speed control and no “Initial-boundary value problem” eyebrow. Domain changes show only applicable endpoint and boundary controls. The three visible domain choices are named “Infinite”, “Semi-infinite”, and “Finite”. Keep View $x_{\min}$ and View $x_{\max}$ together on one shared two-column row for every domain, including the half-line. Boundary-kind selects say only “Dirichlet” or “Neumann”; the adjacent KaTeX data symbol carries $h_L,q_L,h_R,q_R$. Keep editor inline mathematics slightly larger than its surrounding compact uppercase labels. Presets apply atomically in their natural preferred domain: Gaussian Pulse and Square Wave use Infinite, One-Sided Pulse and Forced Wave use Semi-infinite, and Finite standing wave and Mixed boundaries use Finite. Controlled editor renders must preserve the DOM identity, focus, and caret of every unchanged active field while the draft validates and the accepted plot updates. Formula edits validate after a short debounce and the stacked KaTeX card always represents the accepted problem.
- Preserve meaningful landmarks, visible focus, polite live feedback, detailed plot descriptions, 44-pixel controls, reduced-motion behavior, and layouts without horizontal overflow. If WebGL is unavailable, retain the SVG snapshot and expose the accessible 3D notice.
- Keep these stable shell hooks unless a coordinated migration updates application code and browser checks together: `#surface-plot`, `#snapshot-plot`, `#problem-form`, `#problem-formula-math`, `#time-slider`, `#playback-button`, `#characteristics-button`, `#ui-toggle`, and `#restore-ui`.

## Performance And Verification

- Schedule playback and renderer invalidation through `requestAnimationFrame`. Characteristic computation, Three.js object preparation, WebGL submission, CSS2D label submission, and SVG/final-state updates are staged across five separate frames so first-use work does not form a Long Task. Cancellation must cover every stage and roll prepared objects back to the last completed trace. Avoid rebuilding stable Three.js geometry or retained SVG structure on time updates; ordinary plane/slice scrubbing submits WebGL only because the CSS2D/KaTeX labels are static. When accepting a new solution, batch topology, axis-notation, and physical-boundary changes through deferred presentation setters before `setSolution`, so the previous accepted grid is never rebuilt as an intermediate state. Suspend playback on hidden pages and dispose workers, WebGL resources, observers, and listeners during teardown. Browser benchmarks must await loaded fonts and two stable renderer-layout frames, then await a page-owned completion signal with a bounded, non-polling host timeout; never infer completion from a fixed host-side sleep or keep CDP evaluation work inside a measured window.
- Reference targets are worker-solve median below 600 ms, first accepted surface below 2.5 s, animation callback p95 below 16.7 ms, and no measured interaction Long Task at or above 50 ms. GitHub-hosted software-renderer performance is advisory because shared-runner raster and paint times vary substantially; target misses remain visible in the CI log but do not block the tested Pages artifact. Structural benchmark assertions, missing completion signals, browser errors, and malformed measurements still fail CI. Use `BENCHMARK_ENFORCE=1` only on a calibrated performance host. Performance changes require measurement and numerical-equivalence checks.
- Unit tests cover parser precedence/safety, piece validation and zero extension, quadrature refinement, stale worker results, corner compatibility, analytic traveling waves, velocity-only data, all finite D/N eigenmode combinations, driven boundary solutions, reflection signs, causality, energy, reflection caps, and characteristics.
- For UI, renderer, or lifecycle changes, run `npm run test:browser` and inspect a real browser. For numerical, mesh-density, or animation changes, also run `npm run benchmark:browser`. Browser coverage includes all 18 preset/domain combinations, safe scalar endpoint expressions and invalid-draft retention, named-example versus Custom classification, fixed-$c$ symbolic formula/editor mapping, decimal/$\pi$ axis switching, typed and curve characteristic selection, plane/slider/playback synchronization, camera reset, Hide UI, reduced motion, WebGL fallback, desktop geometry, and narrow mobile flow.
